First Day of High School

The alarm blared at six fifteen AM, though I'd been awake since five thirty, staring at the ceiling and mentally rehearsing every possible scenario. First day of high school. New building, new teachers, new social hierarchy to navigate.

Mom insisted on taking photos on the front porch—a tradition she refused to abandon despite my protests. "Stand by the rosebush," she directed, snapping away while I shifted awkwardly in my carefully chosen outfit.

The bus ride was a sensory extravaganza of too-loud conversations and competing body sprays. I found a seat near the middle—not hiding in the back, but not claiming front-row territory either. Strategic invisibility seemed the safest approach.

At the main entrance, student council members in matching t-shirts directed the flow of confused freshmen. A girl with a bright smile handed me a map. "The building makes no sense for the first week," she said. "After that, you'll navigate it in your sleep."

She was right about the confusion. My locker wouldn't open until a passing sophomore showed me the special lift-then-turn technique. I walked into the wrong classroom twice. In the cafeteria, I spent ten excruciating minutes scanning the room before spotting two kids from my middle school.

But there were unexpected bright spots too. My English teacher recommended a book based on a comment I made during introductions. A senior in my computer science elective complimented my coding knowledge. By seventh period, I'd collected four new contacts in my phone.

Walking home, I realized I'd survived. Not just survived—I'd taken the first steps toward finding my place. Tomorrow would be easier. The day after, easier still. High school wouldn't be perfect, but maybe it would be better than I'd feared.
