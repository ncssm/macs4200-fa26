Summer Camp Adventure

The bus rattled down the dirt road, kicking up clouds of dust behind us. Thirty excited teenagers, myself included, pressed our faces against windows streaked with fingerprints. Camp Pinewood was going to be our home for the next two weeks.

"I heard they have a talent show," whispered Maya, my seatmate and best friend since kindergarten. "A full-on musical extravaganza with spotlights and everything."

I rolled my eyes but couldn't help smiling. Maya had a flair for the dramatic, but that's why I loved her. While I packed practical items like bug spray and sunscreen, she'd stuffed her duffel bag with costume pieces and sheet music.

The bus crested a hill, and suddenly we could see it—a cluster of wooden cabins nestled between tall pines, a glittering lake stretching beyond. A group of counselors in matching green shirts waved enthusiastically as our bus pulled into the gravel lot.

That summer changed us all. We learned to build fires, paddle canoes, and identify constellations in the night sky. We stayed up too late and woke with the sun. We made friendship bracelets and memories that would last long after the sunburns faded.

And yes, Maya got her talent show. She performed a surprisingly moving rendition of "Bridge Over Troubled Water" that brought tears to our counselor's eyes. I played guitar for her, my fingers finding the chords despite my nervousness.

Sometimes I still catch myself humming that tune when I'm stressed about college applications or family drama. It takes me right back to that lakeside stage, to the feeling that anything was possible if we were brave enough to try.
