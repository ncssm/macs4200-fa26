The School Newspaper

"Deadline in thirty minutes!" Ms. Rivera's voice cut through the chaos of the newspaper room. Around me, a dozen students hunched over computers, frantically typing or debating headlines.

I'd joined The Weekly Gazette on a whim, writing my application essay the night before it was due. Somehow, they'd accepted me as a staff writer. Now, three months in, I couldn't imagine high school without it.

Today I was finishing a feature on the renovation of the science labs—not the most thrilling topic, but I'd worked hard to make it engaging. I'd interviewed the head of the science department, two construction managers, and several students affected by the temporary relocation of classes.

"Anyone seen the photos from the basketball championship?" called Zach, our sports editor. "I need them for the centerfold layout!"

"Check the shared drive," responded Emma without looking up from her screen. "I uploaded them last night after the editing extravaganza."

This was what I loved about newspaper—the controlled chaos, the shared purpose, the satisfaction of creating something together. We argued over Oxford commas and photo placements with the intensity of professional journalists, then shared pizza and laughed about it afterward.

Ms. Rivera circled the room, peering over shoulders and offering last-minute edits. When she reached me, she nodded approvingly. "Good transition in the third paragraph. You're finding your voice."

Coming from her, those simple words felt like winning a Pulitzer. I made her suggested changes, ran spell-check one final time, and submitted my article to the layout team.

Tomorrow, five hundred copies would appear throughout the school. Students would grab them between classes, maybe read my article, maybe not. But I would know it was there—my words, my work, my small contribution to our school's story.
